part 1 · roadmap

Too many good ideas, no single plan

Marketing, Sales and an external SEO agency each kept their own long list, in different files and formats. Before designing anything, the team needed to agree on what to do first.

01 / gatherCollected every request on one boardCalls with each team, their decks and task lists, one FigJam board
02 / scoreScored impact against effortSame criteria for every item, whoever asked for it
03 / alignRebuilt it around four leadership prioritiesAn 8-week plan; everything else moved to later months
04 / shareMade it something people useA slide deck for leadership and a Notion tracker the team could edit
part 2 · design system

One system under two dozen screens

The provider portal had grown screen by screen, without shared foundations. I set up a system from variables upward and applied it to the existing screens instead of redesigning them from zero.

AI
An AI-assisted workflow, with me in charge of quality Claude built the Figma variables, styles and components through Figma MCP. I set the requirements, reviewed every step and corrected what it got wrong, from the typeface and the size scale to missing components. That let one designer set up a system of this size within a week.
part 3 · dashboard & reporting

Honest numbers for a low-traffic product

Sales wanted one clear dashboard and a single reporting page for providers. Traffic was low, so every number had to be framed carefully. I explored several options and iterated on them with the Sales lead.

  • 1One reporting page instead of two
  • 2Show the numbers, but show declines only as a trend line
  • 3No ranking claims the business had not yet promised customers
  • 4Insight cards built only from data the platform already has, new ones marked BETA

What I took from it

Gathering input is only half the job. A roadmap starts to work once it is tied to the priorities leadership has already set.

With AI doing the execution, my value moved to requirements and review. The system is only as good as the corrections.

With little traffic, how you frame data matters as much as the UI: show what you know, label what you don't.
